Multiple advanced arsenic removal technologies exist, but rural households still depend on low-cost filters for treatment. 
Those filters, built to the same specifications, often exhibit very different breakthrough times, affecting reliability. 
This is partly because the pore-scale adsorption dynamics of the in-situ Iron-Oxide Coated Sand (IOCS) layer, a major arsenic removal zone in low-cost systems like Kanchan filters, are not well-constrained in current designs. 
This study applies a coupled lattice Boltzmann reactive transport model to a Discrete Element Method (DEM)-generated IOCS packing, for single-phase D3Q19 flow and D3Q7 arsenic transport with empirical adsorption kinetics constrained by IOCS data. 
A 3 × 3 × 3 sweep over inlet velocity, influent As (V) concentration, and pH provides global adsorption histories, their time derivatives, and Reynolds, Péclet, Mach, and Damköhler numbers, from which “end-of-adsorption” saturation times are defined using a small-slope criterion. 
The simulations re-stated that the pore-scale Representative Element Volume (REV) approaches saturation on time scales of only a few weeks. 
The muted response to saturation time, even while changing inlet velocity, highlights the need for rate laws that couple adsorption kinetics explicitly to pore-scale velocity and mass transfer. 
This study shows how arsenic adsorption on sand bed may exhaust much earlier in pore scale, and explains why rural filters may show varying reliability in breakthrough.
Pointing clearly for making the pore-scale modelling approach directly usable in designing a full-scale filter instead of just macroscopic models, leading to reliable rural arsenic filters.
